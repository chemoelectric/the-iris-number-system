## The Physical Model: Energy Density Clouds & Wave Refraction

In the Unified Field Theory, all matter consists of localized concentrations of electromagnetic wave energy (matter knots). A galaxy is not an assemblage of Newtonian point masses moving through empty vacuum, but an extended, distributed cloud of energy density:

\[ \rho_E(\vec{r}) = \rho(\vec{r}) c^2 \]

The presence of this localized energy density establishes a refractive index gradient in the vacuum lattice:

\[ n(\vec{r}) = 1 + \frac{2}{c^2} \Phi(\vec{r}) \]

where \( \Phi(\vec{r}) \) is the refractive potential field generated by the total energy cloud distribution:

\[ \nabla^2 \Phi(\vec{r}) = 4 \pi G \rho(\vec{r}) \]

The propagation speed of electromagnetic influences across the lattice is:

\[ v(\vec{r}) = \frac{c}{n(\vec{r})} \]

When a circulating matter knot (star) of finite diameter \( 2 R \) moves through this gradient, the differential wave speed across its diameter steers its center-of-mass momentum vector:

\[ \vec{a} = -\frac{c^2}{2 n(\vec{r})} \vec{\nabla} n(\vec{r}) \approx -\vec{\nabla} \Phi(\vec{r}) \]

### Tidal Forces as Refractive Differential Gradients

When two spiral galaxies approach each other:
1. **Forward Tidal Bridge**: Stars on the near side of Galaxy 1's disk experience a higher refractive delay toward Galaxy 2 than Galaxy 1's center does, drawing them forward into a connecting tidal bridge.
2. **Majestic Trailing Tails**: Stars on the far side of Galaxy 1's disk experience less delay than the center, falling behind the orbital trajectory and unwinding into sweeping, curving tidal tails (like the Antennae Galaxies, NGC 4038/4039).
3. **Energy Cloud Overlap & Friction**: When the two extended energy density clouds interpenetrate, mutual wave deformation produces dynamical drag, causing the galaxy cores to spiral inward and coalesce into a merged elliptical knot surrounded by expanding star streams.

## Unified Application Architecture

The application runs in a unified, widescreen graphical window (`1400 x 800`) operating in a single, stable OpenGL context:

1. **Simulation Viewport** (`1020 x 800` left pane):
   - Shows the two interacting galaxies, their luminous energy cores, rotating spiral disks, and trailing tidal streams.
   - Distinct galaxy coloring: Galaxy 1 is rendered in luminous cyan/ice-blue; Galaxy 2 in warm amber/gold.
   - Refractive equipotential contour rings illustrating the deformation of the refractive landscape as the galaxies approach.
   - Core trajectory history tracing the mutual dance and merger.
   - Real-time HUD showing elapsed time, core separation distance, relative velocity, and star count.

2. **Controls Sidebar** (`380 x 800` right pane):
   - Integrated cleanly alongside the simulation viewport.
   - **Interactive Sliders**:
     - **Galaxy 1 Mass (\( M_1 \))**: Relative energy density mass (0.20 to 2.50 \( M_0 \)).
     - **Galaxy 2 Mass (\( M_2 \))**: Relative energy density mass (0.10 to 2.50 \( M_0 \)).
     - **Initial Separation**: Mutual distance at launch (200 to 750 px).
     - **Impact Parameter**: Pericenter offset distance (-250 to +250 px).
     - **Initial Velocity**: Relative approach speed (10 to 95 km/s*).
     - **Galaxy 1 Tilt Angle**: Inclination of primary disk (-90° to +90°).
     - **Galaxy 2 Tilt Angle**: Inclination of companion disk (-90° to +90°).
     - **Cloud Softening (\( \epsilon \))**: M-res aperture smoothing scale (15 to 70 px).
     - **Simulation Speed**: Time multiplier (0.2x to 2.5x).
     - **Trail Persistence**: Length of historical star stream trails (0 to 50 pts).
   - **Action Buttons**:
     - `RESET & RELAUNCH SIMULATION`: Instantly reapplies current slider parameters.
     - `PAUSE / PLAY`: Pause or resume time integration.
     - `CONTOURS ON/OFF`: Toggle display of refractive equipotential rings.
   - **Interactive Encounter Presets**:
     - **Antennae Prograde Flyby**: Classic prograde pass generating long curving antenna tails.
     - **Direct Penetrating Merger**: Near head-on collision producing rapid coalescence.
     - **Retrograde Collision**: Reverse-spin encounter suppressing tidal tails.
     - **Milky Way & Andromeda Infall**: Scaled massive encounter leading to deep spiral interpenetration.

## Usage

Launch the dual-window application:

```bash
galaxy-collision-refraction
```

Or run via Python module:

```bash
python3 -m galaxy_collision_refraction
```

### Keyboard Shortcuts (Focused on Simulation Window)

| Key | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Space** | Pause / Resume simulation |
| **r** | Reset and relaunch with current parameters |
| **c** | Toggle refractive potential contour rings |
| **t** | Toggle star stream trails |
| **q** / **Esc** | Exit application (closes both windows cleanly) |

## ⚡ High-Performance Cython Acceleration

The core numerical integration loop is equipped with a high-performance **Cython acceleration module** (`physics_fast.pyx`):
- **C-Level Vectorized Gravitational Gradient**: Evaluates Plummer-softened potential gradients and core tidal accelerations in pure C floating-point instructions (`-O3 -ffast-math`).
- **Zero-Allocation Symplectic Verlet Integrator**: Bypasses per-particle Python object lookups, method dispatch, and temporary tuple memory allocations during time steps.
- **Graceful Fallback**: The engine dynamically checks `FAST_PHYSICS_AVAILABLE`. If compiled with Cython/C compiler, it runs at native C speed (supporting 10,000+ stars smoothly); if Cython or a C compiler is absent, it seamlessly falls back to pure Python without error.
