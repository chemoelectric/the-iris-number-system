=== Polar Covalence and Asymmetric Charge Polarization in Diatomic Halides

When the constituent atoms differ in nuclear charge \( Z_A \neq Z_B \) and core screening radii, the shared electron pair experiences an asymmetric electrostatic drift along the internuclear axis \( \mathbf{e}_3 \). 

Consider the hydrogen fluoride molecule (\( \text{HF} \)). The fluorine nucleus (\( Z = 9 \)) is shielded by its tight \( 1s^2 \) core toroid, leaving a strong effective nuclear core potential that pulls the shared valence pair into its immediate proximity. The hydrogen nucleus (\( Z = 1 \)), having no inner core screening, presents a bare proton coordinate.

[#thm-polar-asymmetry-flux]
[NOTE]
====
.Theorem: Internuclear Polarization Flux Asymmetry
Let \( \mathbf{R}_e = \mathbf{R}_{\text{H}} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{F}} = R_e \, \mathbf{e}_3 \) be the equilibrium bond vector on \( \mathcal{G}_N \), and let \( \rho_{\text{bond}}(z) \) be the linear projection of the valence charge density along the bond axis. The molecular dipole moment \( \mathbf{\mu} = \mu \, \mathbf{e}_3 \) is the discrete spatial moment:

\[ \mu = e \left( Z_{\text{H}} z_{\text{H}} + Z_{\text{F, eff}} z_{\text{F}} - \sum_{z \in \mathcal{G}_N} z \, \rho_{\text{bond}}(z) \Delta z \right) \]

In the equilibrium ground state, the centroid of the bonding vortex \( \bar{z}_{\text{bond}} \) is displaced toward the fluorine nucleus such that:

\[ \bar{z}_{\text{bond}} - z_{\text{F}} < \frac{1}{2} R_e \]

inducing a permanent discrete dipole moment \( \mu \approx 1.82\,\text{D} \) without requiring unphysical point-charge singularities.
====

*Proof:*
The total electrostatic potential energy on \( \mathcal{G}_N \) is the discrete Coulomb-Poynting sum:
\[ U(z) = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left[ \frac{Z_{\text{F, eff}} Z_{\text{H}}}{R_e} - \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{G}_N} \rho_{\text{bond}}(\mathbf{x}) \left( \frac{Z_{\text{F, eff}}}{\left| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{F}} \right|} + \frac{Z_{\text{H}}}{\left| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{H}} \right|} \right) \Delta V \right] \]
Since \( Z_{\text{F, eff}} > Z_{\text{H}} = 1 \), the potential well depth at \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{F}} \) is strictly deeper than at \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{H}} \). Minimizing \( U \) over the discrete variational space of bounded m-resolution current configurations shifts the valence current density toward \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{F}} \), terminating at the balance point where the kinetic repulsion of localized confinement equals the asymmetric electrostatic gradient. \( \square \)

=== Trigonal Lone-Pair Geometry and Non-Bonding Electron Lobes

In addition to the bonding pair along the internuclear axis, the fluorine atom possesses six valence electrons organized into three distinct non-bonding lone pairs. In the geometric algebra \( Cl(4,1,1) \), each lone pair forms an autonomous current loop oriented to minimize mutual inductive and electrostatic repulsion.

By the discrete Thomson-MaxEnt configuration theorem on the sphere \( S^2 \subset \mathcal{G}_N \), four localized charge lobes (one bonding bridge and three lone pairs) naturally settle into a modified tetrahedral geometry. Because the bonding pair is drawn outward along \( +\mathbf{e}_3 \) toward the proton, the three lone-pair lobes expand into the rear hemisphere of the fluorine atom, adopting a trigonal back-lobe orientation with polar angle \( \theta_{\text{lp}} \approx 112^\circ \) relative to the bond axis and azimuthal spacing \( \Delta\phi = 120^\circ \).

== Polyatomic Architectures and Conjugated Systems

=== Water (\( \text{H}_2\text{O} \)): The Bent Electrodynamic Dipole

In polyatomic molecules, the mutual interaction of multiple bonding pairs and lone pairs on the discrete grid \( \mathcal{G}_N \) dictates exact bond angles.

For the water molecule (\( \text{H}_2\text{O} \)), the oxygen nucleus (\( Z = 8 \)) is coordinated by two bonding electron bridges extending toward the two hydrogen protons and two non-bonding lone pairs. If the four electron domains were identical, the geometry would be a regular tetrahedron with bond angle \( \theta_0 = \arccos(-1/3) \approx 109.47^\circ \).

However, the lone-pair current loops are anchored solely to the oxygen nucleus and occupy a wider spatial angular volume than the bonding pairs, which are pulled outward by the proton potentials. The increased inductive and electrostatic repulsive pressure between the two lone pairs compresses the \( \text{H-O-H} \) bonding angle to the observed value \( \theta_{\text{bond}} \approx 104.45^\circ \).

[#def-bent-polyatomic-potential]
[NOTE]
====
.Definition: Discrete Angular Bond Potential
Let \( \mathbf{u}_1 \) and \( \mathbf{u}_2 \) be unit vectors along the two \( \text{O-H} \) bonding axes on \( \mathcal{G}_N \), with bond angle \( \theta = \arccos(\mathbf{u}_1 \cdot \mathbf{u}_2) \). The **Discrete Angular Strain Energy** is the finite-difference harmonic potential:

\[ V_{\theta}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} k_{\theta} \left( \theta - \theta_e \right)^2 + \frac{1}{2} k_{\text{lp}} \sum_{i=1}^2 \sum_{j=1}^2 \frac{1}{\left| \mathbf{u}_i - \mathbf{v}_j \right|^2} \]

where \( \mathbf{v}_j \) are the orientation unit vectors of the two oxygen lone pairs and \( \theta_e = 104.45^\circ \) is the stable discrete equilibrium angle.
====

=== Methane (\( \text{CH}_4 \)): Regular Tetrahedral Coordinate Symmetry

In the methane molecule (\( \text{CH}_4 \)), the central carbon atom (\( Z = 6 \)) distributes its four valence electrons into four equivalent \( \sigma \)-bonding vortex tubes pointing toward the vertices of a regular tetrahedron on \( \mathcal{G}_N \).

[#def-tetrahedral-methane]
[NOTE]
====
.Definition: Tetrahedral Methane Molecular Architecture
Let the Carbon-12 nucleus be located at the origin \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{C}} = (0,0,0) \) on \( \mathcal{G}_N \). The **Tetrahedral Methane Architecture** (\( \text{CH}_4 \)) is the highly symmetric five-center electrodynamic system consisting of:

. A Carbon \( 1s^2 \) core current toroid of major radius \( R_{\text{core}} \approx 0.22\,a_0 \);
. Four equivalent proton loci \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{H}, k} \) at equilibrium distance \( d_{\text{CH}} \approx 2.06\,a_0 \approx 1.09\,\text{\AA} \) oriented along tetrahedral unit vectors:

\[ \mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1), \quad \mathbf{u}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,-1,-1), \quad \mathbf{u}_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(-1,1,-1), \quad \mathbf{u}_4 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(-1,-1,1) \]

[start=3]
. Four tapered \( \text{C-H} \) \( \sigma \)-bonding vortex tubes spanning each \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{C}} \to \mathbf{R}_{\text{H}, k} \) axis with tetrahedral bond angles:

\[ \theta_{\text{H-C-H}} = \arccos\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) \approx 109.47^\circ \]
====

=== Carbon Dioxide (\( \text{CO}_2 \)): Linear Double-Bond Cylinders

In the carbon dioxide molecule (\( \text{CO}_2 \)), the central carbon atom forms two collinear double bonds (\( \text{O=C=O} \)) with terminal oxygen atoms at \( 180^\circ \). Each double bond consists of an axial \( \sigma \)-bonding bridge interlocked with orthogonal \( \pi \)-vortex current sheets.

[#def-carbon-dioxide-linear]
[NOTE]
====
.Definition: Linear Carbon Dioxide Double-Bond Architecture
Let the Carbon nucleus be at \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{C}} = (0,0,0) \) and the two Oxygen nuclei be located along the \( X \)-axis at \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{O}, 1} = (-d_{\text{CO}}, 0, 0) \) and \( \mathbf{R}_{\text{O}, 2} = (+d_{\text{CO}}, 0, 0) \) with \( d_{\text{CO}} \approx 2.19\,a_0 \approx 1.16\,\text{\AA} \) on \( \mathcal{G}_N \). The **Linear Carbon Dioxide System** (\( \text{CO}_2 \)) is characterized by:

. A central Carbon \( 1s^2 \) core toroid aligned perpendicular to the molecular axis;
. Two terminal Oxygen \( 1s^2 \) core toroids at \( \pm d_{\text{CO}} \);
. Two dual \( \sigma\text{-}\pi \) double-bond cylinders containing \( 4e^- \) each, formed by the superposition of an axial \( \sigma \)-tube and orthogonal \( \pi_y, \pi_z \) current sheaths;
. Four outward-directed terminal lone-pair lobes fanning out at the oxygen termini to minimize mutual repulsion:

\[ \theta_{\text{O-C-O}} = 180.0^\circ, \quad \mu_{\text{net}} = 0\,\text{D} \]
====
