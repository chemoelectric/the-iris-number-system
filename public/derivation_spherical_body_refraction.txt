1. MOMENTUM DENSITY FUNCTION OF THE BODY
======================================================================

Consider a neutral body of total mass m occupying a spherical volume
of radius R centered at position r_0 = (0, 0, d) relative to a large
mass M at the origin (0, 0, 0).

By the Principle of Maximum Entropy (Least Information), knowing only
that the material is indistinct and neutral without phase boundaries,
the intrinsic mass density is uniform:

    rho_m(r) = rho_0 = m / ((4/3) * pi * R^3)   for |r - r_0| <= R
             = 0                                 for |r - r_0| > R

In the Unified Field Theory, all mass is circulating electromagnetic
activity described by the Master Field Equation D F = J. All energy
is kinetic wave motion. The local energy density at every m-res point
inside the sphere is:

    u(r) = rho_m(r) * c^2

The Poynting momentum density vector of an electromagnetic wave packet
carrying energy density u at local wave speed c has magnitude:

    |g| = u / c = rho_m * c

For a stationary body, the net macroscopic momentum is zero.
By MaxEnt, the direction of internal wave propagation is isotropic
over the 4*pi steradians of solid angle:

    dg / dOmega = (rho_m * c / (4 * pi)) * k_hat

where k_hat is the unit direction vector. The integral of dg/dOmega over
all solid angles yields zero net momentum, while the root-mean-square
kinetic energy flux density matches u = rho_m * c^2.

2. REFRACTION OF A SINGLE MOMENTUM DENSITY VECTOR
======================================================================

A large mass M at the origin creates a localized stress in the field,
yielding an effective refractive index:

    n(r) = 1 + (G * M) / (r * c^2)

The local propagation speed of electromagnetic waves is:

    v(r) = c / n(r) = c * (1 - (G * M) / (r * c^2))

The gradient of wave speed is directed radially outward from M:

    grad(v(r)) = (G * M / r^2) * r_hat

By Huygens' construction and wave mechanics, an inhomogeneous wave
speed creates a transverse velocity gradient across a wave packet,
deflecting its direction of propagation toward the region of slower
wave speed (toward M).

The time rate of change of momentum density for a localized wave
packet carrying energy density u in a refractive index gradient grad(n)
is given by:

    dg/dt = u * grad(n) / n^2 = u * grad(n)

Substituting u = rho_m * c^2 and grad(n) = - (G * M / (c^2 * r^2)) * r_hat:

    dg/dt = (rho_m * c^2) * [ - (G * M / (c^2 * r^2)) * r_hat ]
          = - rho_m * (G * M / r^2) * r_hat

The factor c^2 cancels identically. Every circulating wave packet
experiences a time rate of change of momentum directed toward M,
proportional to the local mass density rho_m and the local inverse-square
gradient, regardless of its local direction of circulation at any given
time step.

4. INTEGRAL OF REFRACTED MOMENTUM DENSITIES OVER THE SPHERE
======================================================================

Let the center of the sphere of radius R be at (0, 0, d) on the z-axis,
with d > R. An internal point is given by:

    r = r_0 + s

where s is the position vector from the sphere's center, with |s| <= R.
In spherical coordinates centered at (0, 0, d):
    s in [0, R]
    theta in [0, pi] (measured from the z-axis)
    phi in [0, 2*pi]
    dV = s^2 * sin(theta) * ds * dtheta * dphi

The distance r from the origin to the internal point is:

    r = sqrt(d^2 + 2 * d * s * cos(theta) + s^2)

The total force integral is:

    F = - G * M * rho_0 * integral_V [ (r_0 + s) / |r_0 + s|^3 ] dV

Transverse Components:
    The x and y components involve cos(phi) and sin(phi).
    Integrating from phi = 0 to 2*pi:
        integral_0^(2*pi) cos(phi) dphi = 0
        integral_0^(2*pi) sin(phi) dphi = 0
    Therefore:
        F_x = 0
        F_y = 0

Axial (z) Component:
    The z-component of (r_0 + s) is d + s * cos(theta).
    Integrating over phi yields a factor of 2*pi:

    F_z = - 2 * pi * G * M * rho_0 *
          integral_(s=0)^R s^2 ds *
          integral_(theta=0)^pi [ (d + s * cos(theta)) * sin(theta) /
                                  (d^2 + 2*d*s*cos(theta) + s^2)^(3/2) ] dtheta

Evaluating the Angular Integral:
    Let mu = cos(theta), so d_mu = - sin(theta) dtheta.
    The angular integral is:

        I(s) = integral_(mu=-1)^(+1) [ (d + s * mu) /
                                       (d^2 + 2*d*s*mu + s^2)^(3/2) ] d_mu

    Substitute w = d^2 + 2*d*s*mu + s^2, so d_mu = dw / (2 * d * s).
    The numerator is:
        d + s * mu = (w + d^2 - s^2) / (2 * d)

    At mu = -1: w_1 = (d - s)^2,  sqrt(w_1) = d - s  (since d > s).
    At mu = +1: w_2 = (d + s)^2,  sqrt(w_2) = d + s.

    The integral becomes:

        I(s) = [1 / (4 * d^2 * s)] *
               integral_((d-s)^2)^((d+s)^2) [ w^(-1/2) + (d^2 - s^2) * w^(-3/2) ] dw

    The antiderivative is:
        2 * sqrt(w) - 2 * (d^2 - s^2) / sqrt(w)

    At upper limit w_2:
        2 * (d + s) - 2 * (d - s) = 4 * s

    At lower limit w_1:
        2 * (d - s) - 2 * (d + s) = - 4 * s

    Difference (upper minus lower):
        4 * s - (- 4 * s) = 8 * s

    Therefore:
        I(s) = (1 / (4 * d^2 * s)) * (8 * s) = 2 / d^2

    Notice that I(s) is strictly constant for every concentric shell of
    radius s.

Evaluating the Radial Integral:
    Substituting I(s) = 2 / d^2 back into F_z:

    F_z = - 2 * pi * G * M * rho_0 * integral_(s=0)^R s^2 * (2 / d^2) ds
        = - (4 * pi * G * M * rho_0 / d^2) * [ s^3 / 3 ]_0^R
        = - (G * M / d^2) * [ (4/3) * pi * R^3 * rho_0 ]

    Since the total mass of the sphere is m = (4/3) * pi * R^3 * rho_0:

    F_z = - (G * M * m) / d^2

In vector notation:

    F = - ((G * M * m) / d^2) * z_hat

The resulting acceleration of the spherical body as a whole is:

    a = F / m = - (G * M / d^2) * z_hat
