[THEOREM]
.Theorem: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic in Cl(4,1,1)
====
Every discrete positive integer measurement count \( n > 1 \) in \( \mathbb{Z}^+ \) decomposes uniquely into a product of prime measurement operations \( n \cdot \mathbf{1}_{Cl} = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{a_i} \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \) up to the order of prime factors.

*Proof:*

. **Discrete Integer Measurement Representation**: Represent integer count \( n \) as multivector scalar \( M(n) = n \cdot \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \) (Postulates 0 & 2).

. **Existence of Prime Factorization**: If \( M(n) \) is prime, the product representation is immediate with \( k=1, a_1=1 \). If \( M(n) \) is composite, it factors into non-trivial discrete measurement steps \( M(n) = M(a) M(b) \) with \( 1 < a, b < n \). By finite induction on integer step counts, every integer decomposes into a finite product of prime counts \( \prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{a_i} \).

. **Cl(4,1,1) Grade-0 Coprimality Lemma**: For distinct prime counts \( p \neq q \), the Grade-2 bivector commutator \( \text{Grade}_2(M(p) M(q)) = 0 \) enforces coprime residue orthogonality in \( Cl(4,1,1) \). If \( p \mid M(a) M(b) \), then \( p \mid M(a) \) or \( p \mid M(b) \).

. **Uniqueness by Parity Induction**: Suppose \( M(n) = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i = \prod_{j=1}^m q_j \). Since \( p_1 \mid \prod q_j \), coprimality forces \( p_1 = q_j \) for some \( j \). Canceling \( p_1 \) and applying induction on the reduced integer step count establishes that \( k = m \) and \( p_i = q_i \) for all factors up to permutation.

. **Tautological Conclusion**: Integer measurement step counts possess a unique prime factor decomposition in \( Cl(4,1,1) \). \( \blacksquare \)

== The Discrete Operational Law of Large Numbers

[#theorem-discrete-lln]
[THEOREM]
.Theorem: Iris Discrete Weak Law of Large Numbers
====
For $N$ independent discrete operational measurement results \( X_1, X_2, \dots, X_N \) on partition grid \( \mathcal{G}_M \) with common Jaynesian expected value \( \mu \cdot \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \), the empirical sample mean \( \bar{X}_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N X_i \) converges in Jaynesian probability measure to \( \mu \) as $N \to \infty$.

*Proof:*

. **Operational Variance Formulation**: Define the discrete aperture variance \( \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^2 = \mathbb{E}_{\text{MaxEnt}}\left[ \|X_i - \mu \mathbf{1}_{Cl}\|_{\mathcal{I}}^2 \right] \), which is strictly finite on bounded grid \( \mathcal{G}_M \).

. **Chebyshev Aperture Inequality**: For any discrete rational threshold \( \epsilon > 0 \), evaluate the Jaynesian prior bound:

\[
P\left( \|\bar{X}_N - \mu \mathbf{1}_{Cl}\|_{\mathcal{I}} \ge \epsilon \right) \le \frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^2}{N \epsilon^2}
\]

. **Main Scale Projection Limit**: Taking Main Scale Projection \( (\downarrow) \) as count bound $N$ increases yields \( (\downarrow) P\left( \|\bar{X}_N - \mu \mathbf{1}_{Cl}\|_{\mathcal{I}} \ge \epsilon \right) \to 0 \).

. **Tautological Conclusion**: The empirical measurement mean contracts strictly to the Jaynesian expectation state. \( \blacksquare \)

== The Vernier Central Limit Spectral Theorem

[#theorem-vernier-clt]
[THEOREM]
.Theorem: Constructive Vernier Central Limit Invariance
====
The standardized sum \( S_N = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N} \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}} \sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \mu \mathbf{1}_{Cl}) \) of independent discrete aperture fluctuations on \( \mathcal{G}_M \) converges under Main Scale Projection to a Gaussian aperture distribution maximizing Jaynesian entropy.

*Proof:*

. **Characteristic Operator Function**: Define the characteristic operator \( \Phi_{S_N}(t) = \mathbb{E}_{\text{MaxEnt}}\left[ \exp\left( \iota t S_N \right) \right] \) using the Iris generator \( \iota \).

. **Taylor-Vernier Expansion**: Expand the exponent to second order over discrete grid steps:

\[
\Phi_{S_N}(t) = \left( 1 - \frac{t^2}{2N} \mathbf{1}_{Cl} + \mathcal{O}\left(N^{-3/2}\right) \right)^N

. **Exponential Limit**: Taking the operational power limit yields \( \lim_{N \to \infty} \Phi_{S_N}(t) = \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} t^2 \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \right) \).

. **Tautological Conclusion**: The limiting distribution is the unique Jaynesian MaxEnt Gaussian aperture density. \( \blacksquare \)

== The Jaynesian Information Conservation Law

[#theorem-entropy-conservation]
[THEOREM]
.Theorem: Entropy Conservation in Closed Aperture Systems
====
Under unitary multivector gauge transformations in \( Cl(4,1,1) \), the total Jaynesian information entropy \( S_{\text{MaxEnt}}(P) = -\sum_{r \in \mathcal{G}_N} P(r) \ln P(r) \) of an aperture probability state remains invariant.

*Proof:*

. **Unitary Gauge Action**: Let \( U \in Spin(4,1,1) \) be a multivector gauge transformation satisfying \( U \bar{U} = \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \).

. **State Density Transformation**: The transformed probability density satisfies \( P'(r) = P(U r \bar{U}) \).

. **Sum Invariance**: Since unitary transformations preserve grid measure \( \mu_{\mathcal{I}}(U \mathcal{U} \bar{U}) = \mu_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{U}) \), the entropy sum satisfies:

\[
S_{\text{MaxEnt}}(P') = -\sum_{r \in \mathcal{G}_N} P(U r \bar{U}) \ln P(U r \bar{U}) = -\sum_{r' \in \mathcal{G}_N} P(r') \ln P(r') = S_{\text{MaxEnt}}(P)
\]

. **Tautological Conclusion**: Jaynesian information entropy is strictly conserved under gauge field evolutions in \( Cl(4,1,1) \). \( \blacksquare \)

== The Maximum Aperture Likelihood Estimation Theorem

[#theorem-mle-uniqueness]
[THEOREM]
.Theorem: Uniqueness of Maximum Aperture Likelihood Estimates
====
For discrete measurement counts on grid \( \mathcal{G}_N \), the Jaynesian MaxEnt likelihood estimator \( \hat{\theta}_{\text{MAL}} \) is uniquely determined and achieves minimal variance on finite operational samples.

*Proof:*

. **Aperture Log-Likelihood**: Define the log-likelihood operator \( L(\theta \mid \{x_i\}) = \sum_{i=1}^k \ln P(x_i \mid \theta) \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \).

. **Strict Concavity**: Evaluate the second MSRA derivative \( D_{\mathcal{I}}^2 L(\theta) \). Under Jaynesian MaxEnt priors, \( D_{\mathcal{I}}^2 L(\theta) = -I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta) \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \), where \( I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta) > 0 \) is the positive-definite Fisher aperture information matrix.

. **Uniqueness of Stationarity**: Since \( D_{\mathcal{I}}^2 L(\theta) \) is strictly negative-definite, the stationary equation \( D_{\mathcal{I}} L(\hat{\theta}) = 0 \) possesses a unique solution \( \hat{\theta}_{\text{MAL}} \).

. **Tautological Conclusion**: Maximum Aperture Likelihood estimation yields a unique, optimal parameter estimate for any finite data set on \( \mathcal{G}_N \). \( \blacksquare \)

== The Finite-Sample Cramer-Rao Aperture Inequality

[#theorem-cramer-rao-aperture]
[THEOREM]
.Theorem: Finite-Sample Vernier Variance Lower Bound
====
The variance of any unbiased operational estimator \( T(X) \) for parameter \( \theta \) on discrete grid \( \mathcal{G}_N \) satisfies:

\[
\text{Var}_{\mathcal{I}}(T(X)) \ge \frac{1}{I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta)} \mathbf{1}_{Cl}
\]

where \( I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta) \) is the Fisher aperture information multivector norm.

*Proof:*

. **Unbiased Expectation Condition**: Let \( \mathbb{E}_{\text{MaxEnt}}[T(X)] = \theta \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \). Differentiating both sides with respect to \( \theta \) using MSRA derivative \( D_{\mathcal{I}} \) yields:

\[
\sum_{x \in \mathcal{G}_N} T(x) D_{\mathcal{I}} P(x \mid \theta) = \mathbf{1}_{Cl}
\]

. **Score Operator Identity**: Rewrite \( D_{\mathcal{I}} P(x \mid \theta) = P(x \mid \theta) D_{\mathcal{I}} \ln P(x \mid \theta) \).

. **Cauchy-Schwarz Multivector Inequality**: Applying the Iris inner product inequality to \( (T(X) - \theta \mathbf{1}_{Cl}) \) and \( D_{\mathcal{I}} \ln P(X \mid \theta) \) gives:

\[
\mathbb{E}\left[ (T(X) - \theta \mathbf{1}_{Cl})^2 \right] \cdot \mathbb{E}\left[ (D_{\mathcal{I}} \ln P(X \mid \theta))^2 \right] \ge \mathbf{1}_{Cl}
\]

. **Fisher Information Identification**: Recognizing \( I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[ (D_{\mathcal{I}} \ln P(X \mid \theta))^2 \right] \) yields the exact lower bound \( \text{Var}_{\mathcal{I}}(T(X)) \ge I_{\mathcal{I}}(\theta)^{-1} \mathbf{1}_{Cl} \).

. **Tautological Conclusion**: No unbiased operational estimator on \( \mathcal{G}_N \) can exceed the precision bound imposed by the Fisher aperture information norm. \( \blacksquare \)
