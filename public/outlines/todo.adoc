  - Secondary to this all is the question of “general artificial
    intelligence.” We are hardnosed and must insist upon a definition.
    Our definition is that the “general AI” must be structurally
    similar to a human.

    * This does _not_ mean it must suffer the same migraine auras!
      That would be the same kind of category error as assuming
      massive networks of anything vaguely resembling neurons would
      produce “general intelligence” by some magical process of
      “emergence.”

    * _We know darn well that neither “consciousness” nor
      “intelligence” is “emergent”!_ +
        +
      Someone will demand, “How do we know _that_?” But is it not
      clear? Merely look at the animal world. These things came about
      by slow evolution. They developed in degrees. They did not
      “emerge.” _They did not “emerge”!_ +
        +
      It is disheartening that there are people who are paid salaries
      at universities to go around theorizing about “emergence” of
      what plainly are the results of slow evolution.

    * LLMs do an excellent job of mimickry but are absolutely empty
      inside and are structurally extremely dissimilar to humans. They
      are very powerful inference engines that operate largely by
      talking to themselves. They would probably operate much better
      as inference engines if done by some other means that were not
      too different, but which did not waste effort mimicking humans. +
        +

  - Disciplined mentation vs. schooling and buzzwords.

    * “Critical thinking” is mostly an empty school buzzword. What is
      actually needed is disciplined mentation.

    * Schools train people to be helpless, repeating formulas and
      believing knowledge is too specialized for an ordinary person to
      understand without an “expert.”

    * Conscious thought can actually hurt physically. It is not some
      weightless game.

    * Having knuckles hit with a wooden ruler to unteach self-taught
      lettering before school: a brutal enforcement of conformity that is
      neither forgotten nor forgiven.

    * Core rules of disciplined mentation:
      ** Put things in correct temporal order: unconscious first, conscious
         second.
      ** Distinguish individuals existing in Nature (organisms) from
         category terms (species).
      ** Keep representations clean: use Maxwell’s equations, avoid
         metaphysical baggage, coordinate-free methods, don’t confuse the
         map with the territory.
      ** Show that any disciplined person can understand fundamental
         matters better than credentialed Ph.D.s.

  - Reasoning backwards (Sherlock Holmes) and childhood reality.

    * Reasoning backwards: look at the fact and trace back to the real
      cause, instead of taking the easy superficial blame.

    * The shoe-tying example: Mrs. G. mocked me for not knowing how to
      tie my shoes at age six. But if an otherwise intelligent six-year-old
      can’t tie his shoes, that doesn’t mean something is wrong with the
      kid—it means his mother was neglectful and didn't bother to teach
      him to take care of himself.

    * Mrs. G. used the wooden ruler for everything, including racial
      prejudice in a mixed-race neighborhood on the Franklin/New
      Brunswick border.

    * But she also had a girl I knew show me how to tie shoes in a simple
      way a six-year-old could actually get, unlike my mother's
      half-hearted complex attempts.

    * And when I had scarlet fever, her son made me a coin bank out of a
