  - Discipline of Mentation vs. Institutionalized Pedagogy and Sloganeering.

    * The distinction between institutional “critical thinking” slogans
      and genuine disciplined mentation. Institutional schooling relies
      on rote authority, specialization myths, and formula recitation,
      instilling reflexive helplessness in students who are trained to
      believe knowledge is too complex for an ordinary person to master
      from first principles without certified gatekeepers.

    * The physical and physiological cost of conscious mentation. For
      intensely focused neurological systems, conscious directed thought
      is not an effortless, weightless exercise but carries real somatic
      strain, sensory overwhelm, and physical pain. Early trauma
      inflicted by institutional enforcers—such as striking a child's
      knuckles with a wooden ruler to suppress self-taught lettering in
      the name of standardized conformity—serves to enforce obedience
      and punish natural, self-directed curiosity.

    * Concrete rules of disciplined mentation to replace institutional
      formula repetition:

      ** Preserving strict temporal and developmental order: the
         unconscious physiological substrate comes first, the conscious
         verbal formulation comes second.

      ** Rigorous semantic hygiene: strictly distinguishing physical
         individuals existing in Nature (such as discrete organisms or
         physical apparatuses) from abstract category terms (such as
         species or statistical ensembles).

      ** Structural and mathematical consistency: refusing arbitrary
         substitutions or mystical dualisms (e.g., maintaining Maxwell’s
         equations without metaphysical baggage, adhering to coordinate-free
         methods, and never confusing mathematical artifacts with the
         physical territory).

      ** Demonstrating that disciplined mentation enables any attentive
         person to grasp fundamental structures without surrendering to
         the myth of hyper-specialization or academic consensus.
