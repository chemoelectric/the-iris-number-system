== Foundations: The Language of Computer Graphics and Kinematics

The geometry is direct, operational, and visual. Geometric entities—points, point-pairs, lines, circles, planes, and expanding spherical wavefronts—are represented as algebraic blades. Kinematic transformations (translations, rotations, reflections) are evaluated directly through multivector sandwich products without coordinate matrices.

=== The Conformal Boundary Terms and Spherical Inversion

In Conformal Geometric Algebra, the points at the origin \( e _ \odot \) and infinity \( e _ \infty \) allow non-linear spatial transformations—such as magnification and spherical inversion—to be treated as linear operations within the algebra.

_Spherical inversion_ is reflection across a sphere rather than a flat plane. Given a sphere of radius \( R \) centered at the origin, a point at position \( \mathbf { x } \) is reflected along its radial line to a new position \( \mathbf { x } ' = \frac { R ^ 2 } { \| \mathbf { x } \| ^ 2 } \mathbf { x } \). This operation turns the space inside-out: points far away outside the sphere are mapped close to the center, points near the center are projected toward infinity, and the surface of the sphere remains fixed. In the conformal basis, this reflection simply interchanges the roles of the origin \( e _ \odot \) and infinity \( e _ \infty \).

\[ D = \nabla + e _ 4 \frac { 1 } { c } D _ t - e _ \infty \partial _ \odot - e _ \odot \partial _ \infty \]

* \( \partial _ \odot \) measures the change in a geometric field under radial magnification or contraction about the origin.

* \( \partial _ \infty \) measures the change in the field under spherical inversion (turning space inside-out toward the boundary at infinity).
