In an isotropic, common-source signal emission, the cross-correlation function \( R ( \theta _ a , \theta _ b ) \) between two directional analyzer channels at orientation angles \( \theta _ a \) and \( \theta _ b \) is strictly a function of the angular difference \( \Delta \theta = \theta _ a - \theta _ b \) and is invariant under any arbitrary translation of the angular origin \( \theta _ 0 \).

*Proof:*
. **Origin Invariance Requirement**: Let \( \theta _ a \) and \( \theta _ b \) be spatial orientation angles relative to a reference coordinate system. Physical isotropy enforces that the channel correlation must satisfy:
\[ R ( \theta _ a + \theta _ 0 , \theta _ b + \theta _ 0 ) = R ( \theta _ a , \theta _ b ) = R ( \theta _ a - \theta _ b ) = R ( \Delta \theta ) \]

. **Signal Processing Derivation**: In digital signal processing and random process analysis, two phase-locked planar multivector wave-packets with opposite relative orientation bivectors \( B_1 = - B_2 \) produce a cross-correlation evaluated by spatial inner product:
\[ R ( \Delta \theta ) = - \cos ( \Delta \theta ) = - \cos ( \theta _ a - \theta _ b ) \]
