== Abstract

Conventional continuum numerical analysis and statistical mechanics suffer from persistent conceptual pathologies resulting from the reification of the mathematical continuum.
Numerical analysis routinely treats computational execution as an imperfect, corrupted degradation of an inaccessible infinite-precision ideal, rather than recognizing finite-resolution discrete operations as the primary operational ground.
Simultaneously, conventional statistical mechanics commits the Mind Projection Fallacy by projecting subjective probability distributions into physical phase-space fluids, while postulating zero-volume point particles and unphysical continuum collisions.
In this paper, we develop a unified, constructive foundation for \( m \)-resolution (\( m \)-res) numerical analysis and statistical mechanics formulated on discrete lattices \( \mathcal{G}_N \) and within the real Clifford geometric algebra \( Cl(4,1,1) \).
We establish aperture-exact operators and finite Vernier convergence bounds that replace arbitrary infinitesimal limits.
Furthermore, we reformulate computational arithmetic: rather than discarding the remainder term of Taylor's theorem as an ephemeral “error” to derive floating-point polynomial approximations, \( m \)-res numerical analysis incorporates the Lagrange remainder directly into the algebraic structure of the computed quantity.
Because Taylor's theorem rigorously proves the existence of an intermediate value without requiring complete microscopic knowledge of its location, the remainder defines an exact, closed aperture interval, enabling deterministic mathematical deductions on the basis of incomplete information.
We then formulate \( m \)-res statistical mechanics where microstates are finite combinatorial grid configurations, and macroscopic distributions are deduced strictly via Jaynesian Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) inference subject to operational multivector conservation constraints.
Finally, we reformulate the Master Field Equation \( D F = J \) in this statistical mechanical framework, demonstrating that the trajectories and phase-space motions of localized field kernels and particles are not arbitrary postulates, but rigorous deductions derived from local boundary flux conservation, finite-duration causal propagation at speed \( c \), and maximum-entropy aperture coarse-graining.

In \( m \)-resolution statistical mechanics, we adhere strictly to the foundational insights of E. T. Jaynes:
probability distributions are not physical fluids; they are explicit, deductive representations of our state of knowledge given incomplete macroscopic information.

Furthermore, phase space is a linguistic entity, not a physical one. It would not exist if no one ever thought it up and wrote it down.

The the order of abstracting is correct: phase space is recognized as _language that talks about_ physical realities, whereas physical realities _are not and cannot be words_.
